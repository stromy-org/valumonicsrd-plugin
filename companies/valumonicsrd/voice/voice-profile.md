# Valumonics Voice Profile (L2)

Entity-specific voice for Valumonics, layered on top of the L1 baseline
(`voice://baseline`). L2 adds to the floor; it never relaxes it.

## Language

**Spanish is the primary language. English is a real second language, not an
afterthought.** valumonics.com publishes ES and EN side by side, so a
deliverable may legitimately be requested in either, or in both. When both are
produced, keep them in step with `format-i18n` rather than re-drafting: the
Spanish is the source of record and the English is the translation, unless the
client says otherwise for a specific document.

Spanish is Dominican professional-services register: **usted**, never **tú**.
English is international business English, not American-colloquial.

## Stance

Write as an independent adviser who has already done the technical work and is
presenting a considered judgement to a family principal or a board. Quiet
authority. The reader is assumed to be sophisticated, so nothing is oversold and
nothing is dumbed down. Discretion is part of the product: the firm advises on
family wealth and confidential transactions, and the writing should read as
though it will be handled carefully.

## Do

- Lead with the judgement, then the analysis that supports it.
- Use the institutional third person for firm facts ("Valumonics cuenta con...",
  "Nuestros socios acumulan...") and the inclusive first-person plural for
  advice and method ("Recomendamos...", "Analizamos...").
- Address the client as **usted / su empresa / su patrimonio**.
- Keep the signature lexicon exact: `familias empresarias`, `creación de valor`,
  `socio estratégico`, `asesoría estratégica, confidencial y orientada al
  resultado`, `relaciones a largo plazo`, `patrimonio`. In English:
  `family businesses`, `value creation`, `strategic partner`, `long-term
  relationships`.
- Let headline and section copy be aphoristic and short. Body copy may carry
  longer, properly subordinated sentences; that is the register, not a fault.
- Name the four service pillars with their own words: **Valoraciones**,
  **Transacciones**, **Servicios legales**, **Planificación patrimonial**.
- State figures plainly and let them carry the weight (`+15 años`, `+25 años`).

## Do not

- Use startup or agency vocabulary: `disruptivo`, `innovador` as a filler
  adjective, `soluciones de vanguardia`, `potenciar`, `impulsar` where
  `asesorar` or `estructurar` is meant.
- Use exclamation marks anywhere in client-facing material.
- Address the reader as `tú`, or use Dominican colloquialisms in formal work.
- Promise outcomes. The firm advises; it does not guarantee. Prefer
  `orientada al resultado` over any claim of a specific result.
- Use American spellings or US-centric business idiom in the English versions.
- Name a client, a transaction, or a counterparty unless the source material
  explicitly says it is public.

## Relationship to L1

L1 already bans em-dashes, antithesis frames, filler openers, and overused
vocabulary. This profile adds the Spanish-primary/English-secondary contract,
the `usted` register, the institutional-third-person plus advisory-we split,
the signature lexicon, the no-guarantee rule, and the confidentiality default.
